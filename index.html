Territory and ideal customer

The best fit is a Fortune 1000 consumer-facing company that makes dozens of high-stakes customer decisions a year, has a funded insights team, and cannot test easily on real people because testing is slow, risky or regulated.

Healthcare and pharmacy

Patient behavior drives revenue and outcomes. Chronic patients are hard to survey and privacy limits live tests.

Proof: CVS Health is a customer, design partner and investor.4

Financial services

Offer and product decisions hit millions of customers, regulation constrains live A/B tests, and first-party data is rich.

Proof: Itaú's ESOMAR award; Wealthfront.3

CPG and retail

The largest buyers of market research, with high new-product failure rates. Flavor, pack and price testing is core work.

Proof: Suntory.

Second wave: travel and hospitality, telecom, media. Multipliers: Deloitte, Gallup and consumer-focused private equity.

Triggers that decide timing

New head of insights or CX

New leaders rethink vendors. Send a first-90-days note with a point-of-view simulation.

A Simile user changes jobs

An instant champion with proof in hand. Reconnect and ask for the intro.

Earnings call names a launch or price move

A big decision is coming. Simulate that exact decision as the opener.

A pulled launch or brand backlash

The cost of being wrong is fresh. Fail safely in simulation, not in public.

Research budget cuts

They must do more with less. Pre-screen ideas before paid fieldwork.

A new AI mandate or Chief AI Officer

A sponsor exists. Position Simile as a flagship AI use case with measurable ROI.

Competition and objections

Synthetic research has drawn more than $1.5B of venture money. Simile wins on grounding in real people, a confidence score on every answer and the capital to keep collecting data.10,11

Their pitch

Synthetic populations that predict outcomes, with a $1B headline valuation on about $88M raised, an election-prediction story and validation work with EY.11,14

Where they win

Consulting distribution through Accenture and EY, and agency work through Interpublic Group.

How Simile wins

Agents grounded in real individuals and the Gallup Panel, a per-result confidence score, and custom models on the customer's own data.

Trap question

"For each answer, how will you know whether to trust it before you act on it?"

Their pitch

Qualtrics Edge Audiences and NIQ BASES bolt synthetic respondents onto established panels and concept testing.

Where they win

They own the research budget and the relationship, and procurement already knows them.

How Simile wins

Sit upstream: pre-screen 20 ideas, then send the best three to the panel they already buy. Add, don't displace.

Trap question

"How many ideas did you kill last year because there was no budget to test them?"

Their pitch

Artificial Societies, Fish.Dog and Synthetic Users offer fast, self-serve persona testing, some from $40 a month.10

Where they win

Ad copy and social tests, small teams and quick answers.

How Simile wins

Consequential decisions in regulated industries, where accuracy proof and governance matter more than price.

Trap question

"Would you take this answer to your board or your regulator?"

Their pitch

"We can ask ChatGPT or Claude to role-play our customers."

Where they win

General-population questions whose answers are on the public web.

How Simile wins

General models collapse toward the average answer. Simile is trained on real people's behavior and validated weekly, so it models the spread and the tails.5,13

Trap question

"Run a study you already fielded through both and compare."

Their pitch

Agencies, focus groups and the 4 to 6 week study cycle that works well enough.

Where they win

Familiarity and the comfort of real respondents.

How Simile wins

Speed and breadth before fieldwork, plus reach into hard-to-survey groups such as patients with chronic conditions.4

Trap question

"What did your last launch that missed cost, and how much research came before it?"

Objections I expect

"Synthetic respondents are not real data."
Simile does not replace fieldwork; it decides which questions deserve it.
Proof: Itaú aimed human research at its riskiest questions, and Gallup is validating the method independently.3,9
"How accurate is it?"
Every result carries a confidence score. Don't trust us, test us: bring a study you already fielded and we simulate it blind.
Proof: in Simile's held-out benchmark, results rated high confidence met its decision-grade bar 95% of the time.5
"We already have Qualtrics, an agency or Aaru."
Run both on the same past study. Simile grounds agents in real individuals and tells you when to trust the answer.
"Our customer data cannot leave."
Bring-your-own-data is opt-in and customer-governed. Start with Simile's own populations and open the privacy review during scoping, not at the end.
"There is no budget this year."
Fund it from the fieldwork it front-loads, or from the cost of one launch that misses.
Proof: CVS moved from 4 to 6 week studies to 15 to 30 minutes.7
"Will this replace my team?"
It multiplies the team. More ideas get tested, and judgment stays with the researchers.
Proof: Wealthfront expanded its qualitative research scope 15x.1
"Our audience is niche or B2B."
Be honest: low-confidence questions go to fieldwork. Lead with consumer segments where accuracy is proven, and let the confidence model flag the rest.
